Submission Strategy

Strategic guidance for maximizing impact and visibility.

Phase 1 — Pre-Release (Before Public Upload)
Offer an exclusive premiere to NOWNESS (requires unpublished work). If declined, offer to Promonews ("One to Watch" spotlight for emerging directors) or Short of the Week (they plan launch dates in advance).
Phase 2 — Release Week
Upload to Vimeo (optimized for Staff Pick: strong title, thumbnail, description, post early in the week). Simultaneously submit to Stash Media, Motionographer, Cartoon Brew, It's Nice That, Directors Notes, and IMVDb.
Phase 3 — Weeks 1-2
Submit to Colossal, Creative Boom, Designboom, Skwigly, Daily Psychedelic Video, Animation Obsessive. Start SubmitHub and Groover campaigns for music-focused curators.
Phase 4 — Weeks 2-4
Upload to ArtStation, Behance, Pixiv (essential for Japanese audience reach), Reddit (r/animation, r/woahdude), GIPHY Artist. Create behind-the-scenes content.
Phase 5 — Awards Season (Months 1-6)
Enter SIGGRAPH 2026, Webby Awards, D&AD, Motion Awards, Communication Arts, Telly Awards, Creative Review Annual. Monitor for Booooooom TV reopening.

  • NOWNESS requires the work to be unpublished anywhere. Submit here FIRST if you want their exclusive.
  • Some festivals prefer or require premieres (world, international, or regional). Check each festival's premiere policy.
  • Sundance & Tribeca have the strictest premiere requirements. If targeting these for 2027, do NOT publish publicly before submitting.
  • Most online platforms (Stash, Motionographer, etc.) do NOT require exclusivity and can be submitted after public release.
  • Recommended approach: Submit to festivals with premiere requirements FIRST, then go public, then submit to online platforms.
  • FilmFreeway has a "premiere status" tracking feature — use it to manage which festivals have seen the work.
  • Video file: DCP version (some festivals require this if selected), ProRes master, H.264 for online
  • Screener link: Vimeo private link with password (standard for festival submissions)
  • Stills: At least 3 high-res production stills (300dpi for print, 72dpi for web)
  • Synopsis: Short (50 words), medium (100 words), and long (200 words) versions in English
  • Director bio: Short and long versions + headshot photo
  • Technical specs: Duration, resolution, aspect ratio, sound format, completion date
  • Credits list: Full crew credits
  • Behind-the-scenes: Process images, making-of content (valuable for press and platforms like Stash)
  • Social media: Trailer or teaser clip (15-30sec) for Instagram/TikTok
  • Accounts: FilmFreeway, Vimeo Pro, IMVDb profiles created and complete

Strategic Advice for a Fully AI-Generated Project

  1. Always disclose AI usage upfront. Hiding it risks disqualification and reputational damage. Transparency is universally valued.
  2. Frame it as "AI-directed" rather than "AI-generated." Emphasize the human creative vision, direction, editorial decisions, music supervision, and artistic intent behind the work.
  3. Prepare a strong artistic statement. Annecy and others require justification of AI use "from an artistic point of view." Write a compelling case for why AI was the right medium for this story.
  4. Prioritize AI-friendly venues first: SIGGRAPH, BIAF Bucheon, Webby Awards, D&AD, Runway AI Film Festival, SXSW, Tribeca.
  5. Music video context is your advantage. Music videos have always embraced experimental techniques. UKMVA and BMVA are likely more receptive than pure animation festivals.
  6. Skip Ottawa (OIAF). Their policy explicitly excludes fully AI-generated work.
  7. Contact festivals with no stated policy BEFORE submitting. A quick email asking about AI eligibility saves entry fees and avoids surprises.
  8. The Oscars now have precedent. Two AI films qualified for Animated Short Film at the 98th Academy Awards (2026). This shifts the landscape significantly.
